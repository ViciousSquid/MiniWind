Title: Songs for a Long Table
Author: As sung at the Wanderer's Rest, and written down by Wynn, who was sober for it

The songs everybody knows the first verse of and nobody knows the second. Here are both, so there is no more arguing.

THE MILLER'S DAUGHTER

The miller's daughter walked to town
With flour on her apron and dust on her gown,
She sold the bread and she kept the pay,
And the miller's still looking for her today.

Oh, the wheel goes round and the stream runs down,
And the miller's daughter's gone to town.

[page]

THE GUARDSMAN'S COMPLAINT

I've walked the road from Kettlewick
To Wyndham Cross and back,
I've caught one thief and one bad cold
And a crick in the small of my back.

The reeve he says, "Be watchful, lads,"
The reeve he says, "Be brave,"
The reeve he sleeps in a feather bed
And I'll sleep in my grave.

[page]

COME DOWN FROM THE HILL

Come down from the hill, the fire is lit,
The ale is poured, there's a place to sit,
The wolves can have the wind and the rain,
Come down from the hill, come home again.

(This one is sung slow, at the end of the night, and if you laugh during it Hollin will put you out in the yard.)

[page]

A NOTE FROM THE INNKEEPER

Singing is free. Standing on the tables is a penny, and so is falling off them. Dancing with the dog is not permitted, whatever the dog says.
