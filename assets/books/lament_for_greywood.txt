Title: A Lament for Greywood
Author: anonymous

The oaks of Greywood stood a thousand years,
grey-barked, grey-bearded, grey as winter rain;
they shaded wolves and woodsmen, fawns and fears,
and never asked a thing of us again.

We took their timber for the mill at Brook,
their bark for tanning and their boughs for fire;
we never thanked them, never stopped to look,
and every year we came, and took them higher.

[page]

Now where the eldest stood there is a field,
and where the field ends there's a road of stone,
and what the forest kept, the forest yields
to plough and cart and to the wind alone.

But walk in Greywood still, at end of day,
when light lies green and low between the trees,
and you may hear them, very far away,
the old oaks talking, slowly, in the breeze.
