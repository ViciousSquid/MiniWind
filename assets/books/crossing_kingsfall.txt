Title: The Crossing at Kingsfall
Author: As told by the Coldharbour family, and set down by Master Corwen

How the Seven came over the mountain wall, and why the pass bears the name it does. The Chronicle tells it in a sentence; the Coldharbours tell it over a whole winter's evening. This is somewhere between.

It was the third year of the famine beyond the mountains. The Seven Families had eaten their seed corn and sold their silver and buried their old, and they had heard from a tinker, who had heard it from a drover, that over the mountain wall there was a valley where the grass grew to a horse's belly and nobody farmed it at all.

They set out at the end of summer, which was a mistake, and they knew it was a mistake, and they went anyway, because they would not live to see another spring where they were. There were three hundred of them and a thousand head of cattle, and a king.

The king was not their king. He was a lord of the low country who had come with them because his own people had turned him out, and who still expected to be called king, and who rode the only horse, and who ate first. The Seven bore it, because he had a sword and some men, and because a quarrel on the mountain would kill them all.

The snow came at the top of the pass. It came all at once, the way it does up there, and in a single night it closed the road behind them and the road ahead. They dug. The king did not dig. He sat on his horse and told them where to dig, and when they did not dig fast enough he told them louder.

On the fourth day, the story goes, Old Ama Fenn put down her shovel, walked up to the king's horse, and asked him to get down and help. He laughed at her. And Ama Fenn, who was seventy, and had buried two husbands and five children, took hold of his boot and pulled.

He fell a long way. The pass is steep there.

The Seven dug their way through in three more days, and the cattle followed, and the horse, too, which turned out to be good at breaking a path through snow. They named the pass Kingsfall, and they named the horse Reeve, and it is said that every reeve since has been told this story on the day of their choosing, so that they remember who does the digging.
