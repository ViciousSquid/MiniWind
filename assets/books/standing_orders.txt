Title: Standing Orders of the Watch
Author: Reeve Halloran, who raised it

To be read aloud to every man and woman who takes the watch's coin, on the day they take it, and again whenever the sergeant thinks they have forgotten.

I. You keep the roads. Not the taverns, not the reeve's hall, not your sister's farm. The roads. A road that is safe to walk is the whole of the Vale's wealth, and the whole of your wage.

II. You carry a shield before you carry a sword. A dead guard keeps nothing. Learn to take a blow on the boss and not the rim, with your arm bent and your weight forward, and you will live long enough to learn the rest.

III. Raise your shield before the blow, not during it. If you are waiting to see where the blow will land, you have already been hit. Watch the hips and the shoulder, and when they turn, the shield comes up. Every time. Even when you are tired. Especially when you are tired.

IV. A thief is to be taken, not killed, unless he gives you no choice. A dead thief cannot tell you who bought what he stole.

V. You do not take bribes. If you are offered a bribe, you will report it to your sergeant, who will report it to me, and I will thank you, and then I will find out why somebody thought you were the kind of guard who might take one.

VI. When the bell rings, you come. Whatever you are doing. Whoever you are doing it with.

VII. You will be cold, and bored, and badly fed, and nobody will thank you. That is the job. On the one night in a hundred when the brigands come down off the Ridge, you will be the only thing between them and the people who did not thank you. That is also the job. Do it anyway.

— H.
