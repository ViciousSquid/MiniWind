Title: A Hunter's Eye
Author: Gerrit Longshanks, Huntsman of Greywood

A plain treatise on the bow, for those who would rather hit what they shoot at.

First: the draw. A bow is not loosed; it is drawn, and the drawing is everything. Draw slowly and fully, to the corner of the mouth, and let the bow do the work. A half-drawn arrow is a wasted one: it falls short, it flies wide, and it will not go through a wolf's hide even if it finds it. A full draw sends the shaft twice as far and twice as true.

Second: the wind. Look at the grass before you look at the deer. The grass will tell you where your arrow will go.

Third: patience. A deer that is walking will stop. A deer that is grazing will lift its head. Wait for it. More game is lost to haste than to bad aim.


Fourth: the arrow. Iron heads for wolves and boar; steel for anything that wears a coat of mail. Never loose an arrow you would not want to fetch, and always fetch your arrows. An archer with an empty quiver is a man with a stick.

Fifth: the quarry. A wolf comes at you; a boar comes at you harder. A bear does not come at you at all until it is close enough to kill you. Learn which is which, and keep your distance from all three.

Sixth and last: practice. There is no book, this one included, that will put an arrow where you mean it to go. Only the bow will teach you that, and only if you ask it every day.
