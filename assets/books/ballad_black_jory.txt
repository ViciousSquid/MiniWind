Title: The Ballad of Black Jory
Author: Anonymous, as sung on Cutthroat Ridge (and, more quietly, in Millbrook)

There are two ways to sing this song. In Millbrook they sing it as a warning. On the Ridge they sing it as a hymn.

Black Jory had a coat of crow
And a knife of Coldharbour steel,
He took the corn from Pennick Farm
And left them not a meal.

He took the silver from the priest
And the bride from the wedding train,
And he laughed upon the Ridge that night
And he never laughed again.

[page]

For Halloran rode up from the vale
With forty men behind,
With forty swords and forty bows
And murder on his mind.

They took him at the ford at dawn,
They bound him hand and heel,
They hanged him at the crossroads tree
With his knife of Coldharbour steel.

[page]

And some say still upon the Ridge
When the moon is thin and black,
You'll see a coat of crow go by
And you'd best not call it back.

And some say still at Hollin's Cross
When the wind is in the tree,
You'll hear a laugh up in the boughs
Where no laugh ought to be.

[page]

A note written in the margin, in a different hand:

He gave the corn to the hill farms that winter, which is more than the reeve ever did. Ask in Furrow End who kept them alive. Ask why the Ridge still sings.
