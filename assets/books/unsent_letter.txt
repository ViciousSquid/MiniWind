Title: An Unsent Letter
Author: Your Mother

To my son Tam, at the watch-house in Millbrook.

The barley is in. Your father says it is a poor crop and he has said that every year for thirty years, so it is probably a good one. The red hen has stopped laying and your father says it is the fox and I say it is the red hen, who was always lazy, like somebody I could mention.

[page]

Your sister is walking out with the Hayward boy. I do not mind. He is clean and he does not drink and he can count. Your father minds, but your father minded when I walked out with him, and he had none of those virtues, so I do not listen.

We heard there was trouble on the Ridge. We heard the watch went up. Nobody has told us anything else. Your father says no news is good news. Your father says a great many things.

[page]

Come home at harvest if they will let you. If they will not let you, come home anyway, and I will tell the sergeant it was my fault, and he can come and argue about it with me in my own kitchen, and we shall see how long he lasts.

Eat. You do not eat. I know you do not eat.

[page]

(Below, in a different, later ink:)

I did not send this. I kept meaning to. The sergeant came instead, in the autumn.

I keep it in the drawer with his good shirt.
