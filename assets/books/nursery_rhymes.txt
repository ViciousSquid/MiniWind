Title: Rhymes for the Nursery
Author: Collected in Kettlewick and Elderbrook

For singing to small ones who will not sleep. Most of them are about wolves. I do not know why that is, but every grandmother in the Vale seems to think it helps.

Hush now, the mill wheel's turning,
Hush now, the hearth is burning,
The wolf's in the wood but the door is shut,
And nobody's coming to our hut.

[page]

One for the miller, two for the mare,
Three for the farmer who isn't there,
Four for the guardsman, five for the priest,
Six for the dragon who wants a feast.

(At "six" you tickle them. This does not help them sleep, but it is the best part.)

[page]

Little lamb on Thornfield hill,
Keep you close and keep you still,
Shepherd's sleeping, dog's away,
Little lamb, don't stray, don't stray.

[page]

Old Mother Nettle lives up on the knoll,
She's got a black kettle and a big wooden bowl,
If you're good she'll give you honey,
If you're bad she'll take your money,
And if you're very, very bad,
She'll turn you into your own dad.

(Do not sing this one if Mother Nettle is visiting.)
