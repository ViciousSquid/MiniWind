Title: The Duellist's Companion
Author: Marisa Vey, Sword-Mistress

Being the first lessons of the blade, as I teach them to every pupil who comes to my yard.

The sword is not a club. If you would hack, buy an axe; it is cheaper and does the work better. The sword is a point and two edges, and the point is the most of it. A thrust reaches further than a cut, it is harder to see coming, and it ends a fight that a cut only begins.

Stand side-on. Your enemy cannot strike what you do not show them. Keep your weight on the balls of your feet and your knees soft. A duellist who is planted is a duellist who is dead.


Watch the shoulders, not the blade. Every blow begins in the shoulder before it reaches the hand. When you can read the shoulder, you will know where the blade is going before your enemy does.

Strike when they strike. The moment a man commits to a blow is the moment he cannot defend himself. The best parry is a thrust that arrives first.

And lastly: the fight you do not fight is the one you always win. I have buried better swordsmen than you who forgot that.
