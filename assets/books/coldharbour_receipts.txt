Title: Coldharbour Kitchen Receipts
Author: Mistress Hale, cook at Coldharbour Farm for forty winters

Plain food for working people, written for my granddaughter, who cannot boil water without setting fire to the house.

HARVEST POTTAGE. One onion, chopped. Whatever is in the garden, chopped. Barley, a fistful for each mouth and one for the pot. Water. Salt. Bacon, if you have it. Boil until the barley is soft and the men are hungry enough not to complain. Serves everybody.

OAT CAKES. Oats, ground fine. Dripping, a spoon. Salt, a pinch. Hot water, enough. Press thin on a hot stone. These will keep a week in a pack and a month in a drawer, and they will taste of nothing at the end of either, but you will be glad of them on the hill.

LAKE TROUT, REEDHOLLOW FASHION. Gut it. Stuff it with sorrel. Wrap it in wet dock leaves and put it in the ashes of the fire. Take it out when the leaves are black. Do not ask a Reedhollow man how long; he will tell you "until it is done", which is true and no help.

PIG'S HEAD CHEESE. No. Not until you are older. Come and watch me do it once, and if you are still standing at the end, I will write it down.

MOTHER NETTLE'S COLD CURE. Honey, hot water, a squeeze of something sour, a splash of something strong. Drink it in bed. If you are not better in the morning, drink another. If you are not better the morning after that, go up the hill and see Nettle herself, and take her a pie, for she will not see anyone who comes empty-handed.

A LAST WORD. A kitchen is a warm place in a cold world. Keep the fire in and the door open and the pot on, and somebody will always come home to it.
