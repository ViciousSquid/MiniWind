Title: A Pilgrim's Guide to the Moonwell
Author: Sister Ilse, keeper of the Pilgrim's Lantern

For those who would walk to the Moonwell and drink, with directions, warnings, and a little of what the water is for.

The Moonwell lies in the hills east of the lake, in a hollow of white stone where the grass grows short and silver. It is said that the first of the Seven to see it was a woman named Ama Fenn, who was dying of a fever, and who drank from it on the night of a full moon and walked home in the morning. Whether the water healed her or the walk did, nobody can now say. The pilgrims come anyway.

The road. Go by the Pilgrim's Lantern, where you will be given bread and a bed and, if you ask kindly, this book. From there follow the white cairns. Each one has a stone set on top by a pilgrim who passed before you; add your own, and do not take one away, for it is very bad luck and also rude.

The hour. Drink at moonrise. Drinking at noon does no harm, but you have walked a long way and you may as well do it properly.

The manner. Kneel. Take the water in your hands, not a cup; the well does not like to be scooped. Drink three times: once for what you have lost, once for what you hope for, and once for nothing at all, which is the hardest and the most important.

What the water is for: not miracles. It is for remembering that you came a long way on your own two feet, and can go a long way further. If it heals you as well, give thanks. If it does not, you have still had a good walk and a drink of the cleanest water in the Vale, and that is more than most people get in a day.
