Title: A Herbwife's Remembrancer
Author: Mother Nettle, of the Witch's Knoll

What grows, what it does, and what it will do to you if you are careless. Written down because the young ones will not come up the hill to ask, and I will not come down it to tell them.

Nightshade. Pretty purple flowers, black berries, a smell like a cellar. Three berries will kill a grown man; half of one, steeped and strained, will let him sleep through the setting of a bone. The difference between a remedy and a poison is how much, and who is counting.

Cave mushroom. The pale ones that glow under the hills. They will give a tired body back its strength for a while, and take it back with interest in the morning. Do not eat them raw. Do not eat them at all if you can help it; brew them, and drink the brew, and be grateful.

Bone meal. Ground from what the barrows give up. It binds a draught together the way flour binds a loaf, and it does no harm to the dead, who are not using it. The priests at Saint Aldric's disagree. The priests at Saint Aldric's do not brew.

Wolf pelt. Not a herb, I know. But the fat scraped from the inside of a fresh pelt carries a salve better than goose grease, and the hunter at the lodge sells them cheap.

Some rules of the kettle:

Never brew with a dirty pot. Yesterday's draught will sour today's.

Taste nothing you have not named.

Write down what you put in, as you put it in, and not afterwards from memory, for memory is a liar and so are you when you are tired.

And: the best physic in the Vale is clean water, a warm fire and somebody who will sit with you. Potions are for when you have none of those, which, if you are reading this, is probably often.
