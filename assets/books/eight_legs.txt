Title: Eight Legs in the Dark
Author: Corin Ashe, cave-walker

What I have learned of the spiders of Webwood, Silkhollow and the hollows beneath, at a cost of one finger, two good lanterns and a great deal of sleep.

They are not ordinary spiders grown large. An ordinary spider waits in its web. These hunt. They have the patience of a spider and the cunning of a fox, and the big ones, the grey-backs, have something like the sense of a dog. They know a lantern means a man, and a man means meat.

They carry things. I have seen it with my own eyes and I will swear to it: a grey-back in the hollow under Webfall dragging a sword in its jaws, a good sword, by the hilt. I do not think they use them. I think they take them from the dead and keep them, the way a magpie keeps a ring. Their nests are full of such things: buckles, coins, a helm, a lantern like mine. If you are brave and lucky you may find a fortune in a spider's nest. If you are brave and unlucky you will become part of the next one.

Some advice, for what it is worth:

Burn the webs before you walk through them. A web is a bell-rope, and every strand rings in the nest.

They do not like fire, but they like the dark less than they hate the fire, and they will come at you through the flames if they are hungry enough.

Look up. Always look up. I lost the finger looking down.

If you hear clicking, and you are alone, and your lantern is guttering, then I am sorry, and I hope this book is of more use to the next one.
