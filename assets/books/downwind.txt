Title: Downwind
Author: Bryn of the High Fold, shepherd

Notes on the wolves of Greywood, and how a body may walk among them and come home again. Written for my nephew, who will have the flock after me, and who stamps like a carthorse.

A wolf hears you long before it sees you, and smells you long before it hears you. So the first lesson is the wind. Lick your finger and hold it up, and if the cold side faces the wood, the wolves already know your name. Go round. It costs an hour. An hour is cheaper than a ewe, and a ewe is cheaper than a leg.

The second lesson is the foot. Put the heel down last, not first. Feel for the stick before you put your weight on it. Moss is quiet, gravel is loud, dry leaves are a trumpet. In the wood, walk where the deer walk; they have been practising longer than you have.

The third lesson is the eye. Do not stare at what you are stalking. Every creature that has ever been hunted knows the feel of a stare on the back of its neck. Look a little beside it, and let it be at the edge of your sight, where the eye is best at seeing movement anyway.

The fourth lesson is patience, and you will not like it. Most of creeping is standing still. A wolf that hears a twig snap will lift its head and listen, and if it hears nothing more it will put its head down again. If it hears you curse, it will not.

Lastly: the pack in Greywood is led by an old grey bitch with a torn ear. She is cleverer than you. She is cleverer than me. If you see her watching you from the treeline, she has been watching you for a while already. Go home.
