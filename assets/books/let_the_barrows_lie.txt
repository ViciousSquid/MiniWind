Title: Let the Barrows Lie
Author: Brother Anselm, of the chapter of Saint Aldric

A plea, set down for whoever finds it, and especially for the young men of Thornfield, who have been digging.

The barrows on Barrow Hill and Cairnmoor are older than the Seven Families. The Seven found them already raised, already grassed over, already old, and the first thing the Seven agreed upon, before ever they argued about anything, was to leave them be. That should tell you something.

What is in them? Bones, mostly. Bronze, a little. Gold, a very little, much less than the tavern stories say. And something else, which our chapter has never named in writing, and which I will not name now. It does not sleep, as we sleep. It waits.

When a barrow is broken, it notices. It does not come out all at once. First the dogs on the nearby farms will not settle. Then the milk turns. Then the cold comes in the evenings, a cold that the fire does not touch. Then, at last, someone sees a light on the hill where no light ought to be, and walks up to see what it is.

We do not get them back, the ones who walk up the hill.

If you have already broken a barrow, do this: put back what you took. All of it, to the last bead. Close the stones. Pour salt across the door and say the names of your dead aloud, so that the thing in the dark knows you have people who will miss you. Then go home, and do not look behind you on the road.

If you have not yet broken one, do not.

Saint Aldric keep you. He did not keep himself, you will notice; his chapel stands in ruins not a day's walk from the hill. That should tell you something too.
